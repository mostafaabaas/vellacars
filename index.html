Guide 1 · Briefing

How to brief a creator for a car review video

A car review video is a logistics operation before it is a creative one. The brief has to settle access, responsibility and claims before anyone picks up a camera. Here is the structure we use:

Access and responsibility — settle these first, in writing

  • Which car, which trim, when — the exact car and equipment level, the filming window, where the car is collected and returned. A creator filming the wrong trim creates wrong content.
  • Who may drive it — only licensed, authorised drivers named in the arrangement; if the creator is not the driver, say who is. Drives are planned on suitable roads and routes, in daylight where the format needs it.
  • Insurance and safety, in general terms — the car is filmed under the dealer's or brand's own insurance and vehicle rules; agree before the day how cover and responsibility work, and brief the creator that safety and traffic law come before any shot — no filming while driving in a way that distracts the driver, no exceptions for a better angle.
  • Condition and care — the car is returned in the condition it was handed over; fuel/charging and cleaning responsibilities are named, so there is nothing to argue about afterwards.

What the creator may and may not claim

Attach an approved claims list: the facts the brand can document — equipment fitted to that trim, warranty and service terms, and official figures taken from the car's own documentation. The creator's honest impressions (comfort, feel, design, space in daily use) are theirs to give; performance, consumption, range or safety claims are only ever stated from the approved list, and comparisons against named competitors stay out unless the brand's legal side has cleared them. If a claim is not on the list, it does not go in the video.

The mistake to avoid

Handing over the keys with a verbal "make something nice". Every dispute in car content — a scratched car, an invented figure, a video published before the reveal — traces back to something that was never written down. Access, driving, insurance, claims, deliverables and usage: one page, agreed before the filming day.

Guide 2 · Creative

Test-drive content that answers buyer questions

Buyers do not watch test drives for adjectives — they watch them for answers. The videos that hold attention are built as a list of the questions a real buyer is already asking, answered on camera:

  • Space, answered by showing. Does a child seat fit behind a tall driver? Does the weekly shop fit in the boot with the seats up? Film the actual test — a person, a seat, real luggage — instead of quoting a litre figure nobody can picture.
  • Consumption, answered honestly. Fuel or energy use depends on driving style, load, weather and road — a single trip's number proves nothing. Good content shows how the car is used across a normal day and refers to the official figures in the car's documentation for the numbers, rather than inventing a figure from one drive.
  • Tech, answered by using it. Pair the phone. Run the navigation to a real destination. Park it in a real space. Screens that only get described stay abstract; screens that get used become a reason to visit the showroom.
  • The ownership questions. What does the service visit look like? Where is the nearest service centre? What does the warranty cover? These are answered from the brand's published terms — and they are often the difference between watching and booking a test drive.
  • End with the next step. Where the car can be seen, how a buyer books their own test drive, which showroom has this trim. A viewer who knows the next step is a visitor; a viewer who does not is just a view.

A note on numbers: this guide deliberately contains no specifications. Every model and trim differs, specs change between model years, and a figure quoted from memory in a video becomes a claim the brand has to defend. The rule we work by: if a number is not in the car's official documentation, it is not in the video.

Guide 3 · Launches

UGC for car launches in Egypt & Saudi Arabia

A launch has exactly one asset that cannot be remade: the reveal moment. Creator content around it works when the filming plan and the secrecy plan are the same document:

What to film at the event

  • The reveal itself — the car uncovered, the first walk-around, the details the brand is leading with (lighting, interior, the feature the launch is built around).
  • First reactions from the segment's creators — the people your buyers already follow, seeing the car for the first time on camera, in the market's own language and terms.
  • The questions corner — creators putting buyers' real questions to the brand's product people on camera: availability, trims, service, warranty — answered from official information only.
  • The context — the venue, the crowd, the market moment. Launch content that shows only a car on a stage could have been filmed anywhere; content that shows the launch places the car in its market.

Embargo discipline — the part that protects the launch

Every creator at the event is briefed in writing, before the event: nothing — no photo, story, teaser or "accidental" background shot — is published before the agreed reveal moment in the market's timezone. Access follows accreditation and the event's own filming rules; creators film where the plan says they film. Content is delivered for brand approval before it goes live wherever the campaign requires it. A leak does not just spoil a post — it can break the brand's regional reveal sequence, which is why the embargo is a signed line in the brief, not a request in a group chat.

Egypt and Saudi specifics

Both markets launch on social first — the reveal moment is consumed on phones before it reaches any showroom. Plan vertical-first filming, Arabic-first creators for the local audience with English versions where the brand runs both, and the market's own calendar (season, holidays, the local motorshow cycle) in the launch date conversation from the start.

06 — FAQ

What automotive marketers ask before their first pack

Who owns the videos — can we use them on our channels and in our ads?

Usage is agreed in writing before filming starts. The brief and quote spell out where the videos may be used — your channels, your paid ad accounts, your showroom screens — on which platforms, and for how long. The usage scope you buy is the usage scope written in your agreement.

Who drives the car during filming, and what about insurance and safety?

That is settled in writing before any filming day: who provides the car, who is authorised to drive it, and how insurance and safety are handled. Filming follows the dealer's or brand's own rules for its vehicles, drives are planned on suitable roads, and creators are briefed that safety and the law come before any shot. No filming day is improvised around a car.

Can creators say anything they like about the car?

No. Every campaign works from an approved claims list — the facts the brand can document about the car: equipment, warranty terms, official figures from the car's own documentation. Creators give their honest impressions of the experience, but performance, consumption or safety claims only come from that list, and anything outside it is cut before publishing.

How long do creators need the car for?

Access is scheduled per video in the production plan — a defined filming window agreed with the dealer or brand, not an open-ended loan. The plan states which car, which trim, where it is filmed and when it is returned, so your showroom stock is never blocked by a vague arrangement.

Can you film at our launch event without anything leaking early?

Yes — launch work runs on embargo discipline: creators are briefed in writing that nothing is published before the agreed reveal moment, filming areas and access follow the event plan, and content is delivered for approval before it goes live. Guide 3 above explains how we run it.

Are prices published?

No prices are published. Packs are structured — a defined number of videos, creators and usage scope — and priced in a written quote after a free consultation or brief, based on the cars, creators and usage you need.
