Creator briefs

Briefing a creator for a car review

A car review lives or dies in the brief. Too loose, and the creator invents facts; too tight, and the video sounds like a brochure. The middle path:

Fix the facts, free the opinion

Lock the spec-sheet facts, the embargo date and the must-show features. Leave the verdict to the creator — that is what their audience is paying attention for.

Name the buyer question

Every review should answer one question a buyer actually has: running costs, family space, city parking. One question per video keeps it finishable.

Agree the honest bit in advance

Decide before shooting which drawbacks can be said on camera. Agreed honesty reads as confidence; censored praise reads as an ad.

Test drives

Test-drive content that feels real

A test drive is the closest a buyer gets to ownership before the showroom. Filmed right, it does the showroom’s job early.

Drive where buyers drive

School run, ring road, parking garage. The route is the review — buyers watch for their own streets, not a closed track.

Film the small frictions

Boot loading, car-seat fitting, screen glare at noon. Small frictions are what owners live with — and what makes the content believable.

Keep the first reaction

The creator’s first cold-start impression is the most trusted ten seconds of the video. Protect it; don’t reshoot it into an ad.

Launches

Launch content under embargo

Launch content is a secrecy operation with a publish button. The discipline is simple, and it is non-negotiable:

Brief under NDA, in person

Embargoed details go to signed creators only, with the lift time written down — date, hour and timezone.

Shoot sealed, store sealed

No personal phones on set, footage on production drives only, nothing posted — not even a teaser — before the lift.

Publish at the minute

Edits are approved before reveal day, so creators publish at the embargo minute — not an hour of editing after it.

UAE market

Car content for the UAE: reviews and launches between Dubai and Abu Dhabi

The UAE buyer researches in two languages and two cities at once. Car content that works here is planned as one production system, not two campaigns:

Gulf-spec is the story

UAE buyers ask Gulf questions first: air-conditioning under real summer heat, desert and highway composure, and warranty and service coverage in their emirate. A review that answers those on camera — in the heat, not in a studio — is worth more than any spec-sheet reading.

Two languages, filmed as two originals

Arabic and English cuts are commissioned separately: creators who speak to Emirati and Gulf audiences in Arabic, and to the UAE's English-speaking residents in English — same car, same day, two native scripts rather than one translation.

One calendar for Dubai and Abu Dhabi

Launches, dealer events and test-drive days are planned on a single calendar serving both cities, with embargo discipline shared across them — so no creator publishes a Dubai reveal while Abu Dhabi is still sealed.

FAQ

What automotive marketers ask first

Straight answers, before the first call.

Do creators say whatever they want about the car?

They give their honest opinion — that is why their audiences trust them. What is fixed in advance is factual accuracy: every specification claim is checked against the manufacturer’s published data before the video ships.

Who provides the cars for the shoots?

Cars are provided by the brand or dealer for the shoot days, under the arrangements agreed in the brief. Creators never review their own car as a paid review of that car.

Can content be ready for our launch date?

Yes — launches are the most planned work we do. Creators are briefed under NDA, content is shot and approved before reveal day, and everything publishes at the embargo minute.

What do we receive at the end?

The finished videos in vertical and horizontal cuts, captions, and written usage rights for your own channels and ads — agreed in the brief before shooting starts.

Which markets do you cover?

Egypt and Saudi Arabia — creators, locations and shoot logistics in both markets, in Arabic and English.
